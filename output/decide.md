**Decision:** The Opposition (against the motion) is more convincing.

**Why:**
The opposing side presented a more logically sound and nuanced argument regarding the nature of different human relationships. 

The proposer's argument relies heavily on a transactional view of friendship—that because physical presence and daily labor are provided, it entitles the friend to a higher hierarchical status of loyalty and priority over a romantic partner. While the proposer correctly highlights the value of physical proximity and real-world support, demanding that this translate into outranking a romantic partner introduces unhealthy conditions to friendship. 

Conversely, the opposition successfully demonstrated that friendship and romantic partnerships serve fundamentally different, non-competing roles in a person's life. The opposition's argument is stronger because it points out that genuine care and support should not be conditional or transactional ("with a transactional invoice"). Furthermore, it rightly emphasizes that a long-distance relationship involves a unique emotional intimacy and a future-oriented commitment that physical distance does not invalidate. Therefore, the opposition's stance that friends should support each other to thrive in all areas of life—including romance—rather than demanding supremacy over a partner, is the more mature and convincing position.
